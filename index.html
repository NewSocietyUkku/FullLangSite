00pronunciationOfSounds.txt

Stagement sounds k/t/m/n k is like 'kilo' in English. f is like 'Freddy' in English. n is said like 'november' in English. p is said like 'peter' in English. Bridging sounds u is said like 'blue' in English. o is said like 'orange' in English. e is said like 'egg' in English. a is said like 'ugly' in English. i is said like 'bit' in English. Hidden sounds r is said like 'red' in English. l is said like 'lion' in English. rr is said like 'water' in English. (It is optional to keep it as a rolled R if speakers find that easier, but the official way is with a 'w'.) Suffix sounds y is said like 'yankee' in English. Defining sounds t is said like 'tango' in English. g is said like 'golf' in English.

1definingSounds.txt

The defining sounds (Keep in mind that whether it is gu, gi, etc. is purely determined by the pronoun attached to the word.) 1. gif tells the relationship of one object to another (e.g. above, in front of, etc.) 2. gin the state or its government (e.g. Germany went to war with France) 3. gip the land (physical land owned by the government), e.g. Africa, Europe, etc. 4. Gik land controlled by the state (e.g. if you say France with this defining sound you mean the physical land owned by the French government rather than France as a geographic region) 5. tif names of animals and body parts (also used for things derived from that, e.g. food from a plant or wood from a tree) 6. tin if the thing describes time, weight, size or quantity e.g. day, month, kilogram, etc. 7. tip all others 8. tik emotions (remember that it is not 'I love you' but 'I feel love because of you') beity is an emotion. Something isn't beautiful; rather, it is a feeling you get when you see something beautiful. 9. a d or b is used, whichever the speaker feels most comfortable with d/bi names that have been converted to itti 10. d/bim the sound used to show the ender for both the d/bin and d/bit defining sounds e.g. d/bin lili d/bim 11. d/bin words used from other languages names (e.g. Lili, Fanta, etc.) 12. d/bit words taken from other languages e.g. if you want to say hund is the German word for dog in itti, you'd say d/bit hund d/bim 13. d/bik onomatopoeia (e.g. ring ring for cellphone)

3suffixSounds.txt

You need to add more suffix sounds as you practice converting the language, to see what suffix sounds are needed and what are not. 0. yes this defining sound shows that a sentence has ended. 00. yi, yu, ye, yo, ya (used to show the end of a sentence, e.g. yi-yi OR can-ya; or used just to add an extra syllable to apply the rule that all words need to have an even number of syllables) 1. yif possible/conceived of 2. yifs con/with/have (in the sense of using) e.g. I eat food with chopsticks I am using the chopsticks to eat food or I am happy with you I am using you to become happy (this is different from because) 3. yip something is yours as in you are currently using it (e.g. using a ladder for a day before returning) 4. yips something is not yours (e.g. but belongs to the bank); use this as "mine" but with yins = mine 5. yun being part of something or being there (e.g. my company) 6. yuns shows a relationship e.g. my other, son, etc. 7. yuk being born in (e.g. German because you were born in Germany, or a cellphone is Korean because it was made in Korea) 8. yuks shows a history relationship, a relationship created due to having a shared history e.g. I'm English because I lived in England before 9. yerf prevent/save 10. yerfs responsible for/because of 11. yerp implying something 12. yerps (not) 13. yorn -er (e.g. bigger) 14. yorns most 15. york long extent 16. yorks short extent 17. yalf compliment 18. yalfs insult 19. yaln change from 20. yalns change to 21. yus-fuf information is gathered first-hand (e.g. I saw the ball myself) 22. yus-nun information gathered is second-hand [got it from someone who got it first-hand] 23. yus-pen information gathered is tertiary-hand [not gathered second-hand or first-hand] 24. yus-kef information gathered is either second-hand or tertiary-hand 25. yus since you always use the male form and it is gender-neutral, you never translate, for example, 'madre' but only translate 'padre' since padre is both mother and father, you use yus to specify that it is female tif-kak: fak--re = parent tif-kak: fak--re:: yus = female 26. yus-sus this specifies that it is male e.g. tif-kak: fak--re = parent tif-kak: fak--re:: yus-sus = male 27. yas-kres in general or generally 28. yi-pas shows that something is the focus of the sentence 29. yos to ask a question 30. yos-kan (rhetorical) 31. yos-ro why e.g. yos-kran (rhetorical why) 32. yos-le when yos-klen (rhetorical when) 33. yos-wu where 34. yos-lal how 35. yos-pep asking, but if you say no it is okay if you do not give an answer 36. yos-tuk demanding an answer from the person

4wordForms.txt

There are 5 word forms. If you want to describe a cat as having a slightly black colour, the words cat and black are said in the first form, and the second form is given to the word 'slightly' after 'black' to show it is describing a specific quality of the blackness and not the sentence as a whole. e.g. black[1]slightly[2]cat[1] There are 5 levels to this, and if you want to describe something further than the 4th level, you need a new word and start again at the first level. The defining sounds show you what word form it is. 1. 1st word form gif 2. 2nd word form glif 3. 3rd word form grif 4. 4th word form gilf 5. 5th word form grilf

5wordTenses.txt

To add a tense to a word, you simply add a stagement sound to the end of a word. 1. word-f (future tense) 2. n word-n (past tense) 3. word-k (present tense) 4. word-prr (habitual tense) By default, none of these tenses above need to be added to a word if the user feels no need to add them. However, the itti language makes a distinction between 'I am currently doing something', 'I was just about to', and 'I just finished'. The speaker has to choose one of these forms, and the forms are shown inside the defining sound. The 3 forms are shown below. 1. gif (busy happening, e.g. I am killing the man) 2. gif-t, tif-t [please note that this does not change the tense of the word (e.g. future tense, past tense, etc.), as 'if' is found at the start of the word as opposed to the end of the word] has just been completed, e.g. I just finished killing the man / I have just killed the man 3. gif-it OR tim-it (always the same bridging sound for both syllables) was just about to do something e.g. I was just about to kill him

6wordStress.txt

All words in itti need to have an even number of syllables (e.g. 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, etc.). This number changes by adding suffix sounds, etc. If the number of syllables is an odd number (e.g. 1, 3, 5, 7, etc.), it needs to add an extension at the end to make it even. e.g. ti-ko: fon (chicken) cannot be said and needs to change to something like ti-ko: fon--ya The way stress is assigned is as follows: 1. first syllable stressed 2. second syllable unstressed 3. third syllable unstressed 4. fourth syllable stressed and then it repeats again after that, with the 5th syllable acting as syllable 1, the 7th syllable as the 2nd, etc. Since word stress doesn't change the meaning of the word, what counts as stress is flexible. A speaker can use whatever they feel comfortable with, whether that be like a Proto-Indo stress or a tonal change, etc.

7endingSentences.txt

In itti, to show the end of a sentence, you have two separate methods. 1. To use the 'yes' suffix sound that is added to the end of a word to show that the sentence has finished 2. To add a ya/yo/yu/ye/yi when it is the same as the last bridging sound of the last syllable in a word e.g. pi-kek: llo--yo (the o sounds are the same for 'llo' and 'yo')

8numbers.txt

The itti language uses a base 900 number system. However, in reality, this is done with a base 30 number system, where two 30 numbers are combined to get 900. In reality, a speaker of the new language can easily keep combining these to extend the base of the number system to get higher and higher bases (but 900 is the default assumed number system). For example, 30 times 30 times 30 = 27 000 (which is the number system when three 30 values are used as one value). If you wanted a 2600, you just removed the last 100 digits. Therefore, the language allows you to work with any base number system. The way numbers are shown is that the syllable starts with a click sound (any click sound will do, as the language is flexible; this is how you show a number). After the click sound is a bridging sound or an n followed by a bridging sound. The n is optional if the speaker feels it is easier to speak that way. e.g. click + 'at' vs click + 'nat' The way you would distinguish 11 (901) vs 1-1 (31) is by separating the syllable into two: click + new syllable + 'nat' = 901 click + 'nat' = 31 The way you distinguish 3, 4 from 34 is simple: you just add a double click. click+of click+new syllable click+af 3, 4 The numbers are shown below. click+uf=1 click+ef=2 click+of=3 click+af=4 click+if=5 click+ufs=6 click+efs=7 click+ofs=8 click+afs=9 click+ifs=10 click+un=11 click+en=12 click+on=13 click+an=14 click+in=15 click+uns=16 click+ens=17 click+ons=18 click+ans=19 click+ins=20 click+up=21 click+ep=22 click+op=23 click+ap=24 click+ip=25 click+ups=26 click+eps=27 click+ops=28 click+aps=29 click+ips=0 You add, multiply, power of, etc. L to get plus. e.g. click+aps click+uLf click+aps 29+29 click+aps click+eLf click+aps 29*29 click+aps click+oLf click+aps 29 to the power of 29 Negatives are shown with an r. You add, multiply, power of, etc. an r. e.g. click+aps click+urf click+aps 29-29 click+aps click+erf click+aps 29/29 click+aps click+orf click+aps 29 to the square, cube, etc. of 29 = is said by positive to zero click+iLts zequals (more or less) is two equals put together click+iLts click+iLts To show a number is negative, you simply add a zero negative to the end of a number. e.g. click+aps click+ips negative 29 10/2=5 — this is the assumed order, but if you put the negative twice, the order changes to 2//10=5. e.g. click+ef click+erf click+erf click+ilps click+ifs Lastly, like how you can use x, y, z in English as substitute variables, so too does this language have variables inside speech. click+up=a click+ep=b click+op=c click+ap=d click+ip=e click+ups=f click+eps=g click+ops=h click+aps=i click+ips=j e.g. a=2*j click+up click+ilps click+elf click+elf click+ips Syllable stress rules and dealing with numbers: you can stress and non-stress whichever syllables you want as a speaker. If you want to describe a number with a word form, just place the word form and the number directly after it (it is obvious that it is not a new word because no word can only have a defining sound). e.g. gri click+'un' Numbers can also be translated directly from Spanish into the new language e.g. uno, etc. Please see section 9 for further details on how this process works.

9convertingSpanishToItti.txt

To convert a Spanish word to itti, you need to understand the following: 1. All syllables are translated as is by translating the bridging sound, stagement sound and hidden sound. If you have a diphthong, an extra syllable is added, or if you have 2 consonants, then an extra syllable is added. The vowel stays the same for each syllable made. e.g. trampa = tram-pa but tram has 2 consonants, so it changes to tra-am-pa. io = i-o 2. All vowels (bridging sounds) in the word stay the same, as well as all l/r sounds. e.g. trampa therefore ra-a:a stays the same 3. Double r sound (remember it is about the sound, not the spelling; this rule applies to words like rapido because it is pronounced as rrapido) is said like w. If you have a syllable where it appears after the bridging sound, then it is removed entirely. pe-rro is allowed (pe-rro) but perr-tra changes to pe-tra as it cannot appear after the bridging sound inside a syllable. [something to keep in mind, unlike the others above, e.g. n, k, ll, etc.: r, l and rr do not get converted when in the new language. Instead, they stay in the same place in the syllable. For example, 'perro' changes to 'pi-kek: RRo-ya', 'pi-kek: LLo-ya'. Notice that the syllable 'rro' in 'pe-rro' stays the same in 'pi-kek: rro-ya', the 'rro' sound.] 4. ll becomes e llama = [e]ama 5. Spanish n becomes h niña = niha 6. All h is removed from a word hablar = ablar Always take the standard version of a word (a verb) e.g. hablar and not hablamos (a hablar) 7. Change 'ch' to 'a' e.g. chico = aico (this is the same concept as ll to e; since bridging sounds do not get converted, there is no risk of confusion) 8. Because R/L are not removed, they can be removed and the list of numbers can be shrunk from 26 to 24 letters. The last two letters of the alphabet are changed to L and R. y = L z = R 9. Only words that you can grammatically say 'un/una/el/la' or 'mi' with are allowed to be converted. e.g. 'mi agua' is grammatically correct; 'un mes' is grammatically correct 'mi hablando' — wrong grammar 'el hablando' — wrong grammar therefore you cannot convert the word hablando into itti Below is a system to convert words. Each word is converted based on the alphabet and then a value is added in. e.g. m + bridging sound + m = 1 Below is a complete list as well as the translation of trampa: tra-am-pa (tip defining sound because trampa is something intangible) t = 20 = ka m = 13 = fak p = 16 = kak therefore trampa is def sound + ko + fok + kok therefore it is tip-kra: fak--kak Notice that 'fa' and 'af' are represented in exactly the same way in itti. The full list to convert is below (try to create an HTML program that will convert all this for you more quickly than using the list. Also notice that because vowels aren't removed, you will never need to convert them to numbers, so some of this list is not used). (Please keep in mind that the bridging sound 'vowel' changes to match whatever it is in the Spanish word.) 1. fof: a 2. nof: 3. pof c 4. kof: d 5. fon: e 6. non: f 7. pon: g 8. kon: h 9. fop: i 10. nop: j 11. pop: k 12. kop: l 13. fok: m 14. nok: n 15. pok: o 16. kok: p 17. fo: q 18. no: r 19. po: s 20. ko: t 21. of: u 22. on: v 23. op: w 24. ok: x

10decreasingSyllables.txt

When speaking, many syllables can make speaking longer. For this reason, words can be shortened as the defining sound helps to identify the word. e.g. trampa = tip-kra: fak-kak If a word uses 's' at the start of the syllable, then it means it's been shortened. e.g. tips-kra = shortened version, and the 's' in 'tips' lets you know it's shortened. Therefore you don't need an extra sound for rhythm balance because you can always shorten this to 2 syllables. e.g. tips-yak Now the tips-kra changes to tips as an extra syllable is added to yak. This is for if the syllables removed are two. If you are removing only one syllable, then you add s at the start of the word. e.g. stik-fan stik-fan: ton-ye Words are always shortened to even syllables. e.g. a 4-syllable word will be shortened to 2 syllables, and a 3 to 2 syllables. If you add in an extra sound such as yak, then it always shows to the full extent. e.g. the 3-letter word shortened will stay 3 syllables e.g. gut-kra-tan = guts-kra and guts-kra + yun = gut-kra-tan-yun The same concept applies to 4 syllables, which are normally 2. If you add another sound such as yen, it becomes 3 syllables plus the sound (the rule is simple: if a shortened sound has another sound, it needs to add an extra syllable; it always adds one that shows more of the original Spanish word).

12convertingTheScriptToComputerLang.txt

As mentioned in the previous document, there is no need to create a new Unicode arrangement for this new language, as the current Unicode representation of characters is sufficient to represent these characters. This document will specifically explain how such a language is represented using a normal typing keyboard found on any laptop or computer or a virtual keyboard found on any smartphone. The following works on any English or Spanish QWERTY keyboards. For other language keyboards, such as German, typing may become less fluid and therefore more difficult. Firstly, the direction of 'left to right' or 'right to left' does not need to be represented, as that is determined by page size and therefore it is up to the programs converting this computer data into text to be displayed on the screen to decide which direction the text is to be displayed in. Some keyboards allow for two buttons to be pressed at the same time, and it is these that are the norm and ideal for writing, and the software when writing will automatically write out the characters correctly. If you only have a keyboard that writes one character at a time that is also fine, but it means that typing will become slower. The rule for representing characters that are two pressed together is that right is before left. All characters are made from combining only [q, a, w, s, d] with [u, j, i, k, o, l]. Empty spaces do not need to be represented as the software can do that automatically. For example, if you have a two-syllable word and then a defining sound, the program automatically knows you need two empty spaces because the rules of itti say that a defining sound can only appear at the start of a word and the stress pattern needs to be reset back to its normal start. Defining sounds Numbers are written with normal base-9 numbers (this is to save space), and plus/minus etc. is shown by adding a + or - at the start, and variables are shown by adding an = at the start of the number. However, when typing it out the software would be clever enough to convert the above, and how you actually type it is by using double qawsd combined with double ujikol: double u + double qawsd = 1 to 5 e.g. u + u + q + q = 1 (on keyboards where you can tap two characters at once it is only u + q; the reason for this is that the difference between u and then q pressed and with them being pressed at the same time is easily done by the computer) u + u + a + a = 2 u + u + w + w = 3 u + u + s + s = 4 u + u + d + d = 5 double j + double qawsd = 6 to 10 double i + double qawsd = 11 to 15 double k + double qawsd = 20 to 25 double o + double qawsd = 26 to 29 and then 0 double l + q = plus sound double l + w = minus sound double l + d = character sound for variables Any e combinations are used for computer functions, e.g. e + u = enter, etc.

14wordsMoreThan4Syllables.txt

If a word happens to be greater than four syllables, then you do not convert it the previous way but instead do the following. Remove all hidden sounds from the Spanish word (you do not really need them, as there is enough context in the words to tell them apart). Secondly, to represent two syllables as one syllable: the first bridging sound (vowel) stays the same and is represented the same; the second bridging sound is represented by a bridging sound at the end of the word. a = B e = D i = K o = N u = F So 'i-a', for example, would become 'ib'. Now for the first consonant it is represented by a combination of an s/sh sound plus a stagement sound, and for the second it is represented by a combination of stagement sounds and an s/sh sound at the end of the word. The method is still the same as previously for the 24 sounds, but just with different sounds. 1. fof: a = B = no hidden sounds 'a' 2. nof: b = D = 'ar' 3. pof: c = K = 'al' 4. kof: d = N = 'wa' 5. fon: e = f = 'war' 6. non: f = t = 'wal' 7. pon: g = m = 'la' 8. kon: h = sB = 'lar' 9. fop: i = sd = 'lal' 10. nop: j = sk = 'ra' 11. pop: k = sn = 'rar' 12. kop: l = sf = 'ral' 13. fok: m = st = 'as' 14. nok: n = sm = 'ars' 15. pok: o = shB = 'als' 16. kok: p = shD = 'was' 17. fo: q = shK = 'wars' 18. no: r = shn = 'wals' 19. po: s = shf = 'las' 20. ko: t = 'shT' = 'lars' 21. of: u = 'shM' = 'lals' 22. on: v = 's' = 'ras' 23. op: w = 'sh' = 'rars' 24. ok: x = 'z' = 'rals' Please note that s forms of the defining sound still count as shortening, and you would remove one or two syllables from the last syllable of these words the same way as before using the 's' to shorten it.
